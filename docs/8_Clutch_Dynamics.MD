
# 8. Clutch Dynamics and Slip Mechanics

The powertrain force is generated by the engine and transferred across the clutch assembly
to the transmission input shaft. In this framework, the clutch acts as a friction-based
dynamic interface operating in a dual-branch architecture: a locked direct-drive regime
and a torque-limited slipping regime.

---

## 8.1. Torque-Limited Driveline Transfer

When the clutch is slipping during vehicle launching or stall prevention, it acts strictly
as a torque limiter. The torque transmitted to the transmission input shaft cannot exceed
the applied clutch clamping capacity. The drive torque delivered to the wheels is governed by:

$$T_{\text{wheel}} = T_{\text{clutch}} \cdot i_{\text{gear}} \cdot i_{\text{final}}, \tag{8.1.}$$

where:
* $T_{\text{wheel}}$ is total wheel torque in Newton-meters (N·m),
* $T_{\text{clutch}}$ is transmitted clutch torque capacity in Newton-meters (N·m),
* $i_{\text{gear}}$ is active gear ratio which is dimensionless,
* $i_{\text{final}}$ is final drive ratio which is dimensionless.

---

## 8.2. Engine Rotational Dynamics during Slip

During clutch slip, the engine crankshaft is mechanically decoupled from the transmission input shaft.
The excess torque resulting from the imbalance between engine output and clutch torque drives the
rotational acceleration of the engine crankshaft assembly:

$$\frac{d\omega_{\text{engine}}}{dt} = \frac{T_{\text{engine}} - T_{\text{clutch}}}{I_{\text{engine}}}, \tag{8.2.}$$

where:
* $\omega_{\text{engine}}$ is engine rotational speed in radians per second (rad/s),
* $T_{\text{engine}}$ is generated engine torque in Newton-meters (N·m),
* $T_{\text{clutch}}$ is transmitted clutch torque in Newton-meters (N·m),
* $I_{\text{engine}}$ is engine and flywheel rotational inertia in kilogram square meters (kg·m²).

---

## 8.3. Frictional Thermal Dissipation

The difference in rotational speed across the slipping friction plates converts mechanical energy
directly into thermal dissipation rather than kinetic vehicle motion:

$$P_{\text{heat}} = T_{\text{clutch}} \cdot \left| \omega_{\text{engine}} - \omega_{\text{trans\_input}} \right|, \tag{8.3.}$$

where:
* $P_{\text{heat}}$ is thermal dissipation power in Watts (W),
* $T_{\text{clutch}}$ is transmitted clutch torque in Newton-meters (N·m),
* $\omega_{\text{engine}}$ is engine rotational speed in radians per second (rad/s),
* $\omega_{\text{trans\_input}}$ is transmission input shaft rotational speed in radians per second (rad/s).

---

## 8.4. Architectural Notes and Real-World Limitations

[!WARNING]
The equations defined in this module represent a baseline, torque-limited slip model suitable
for a Minimum Viable Product (MVP). They assume ideal clutch modulation, instantaneous pressure
application, and a constant friction coefficient. In high-fidelity vehicle dynamic models, clutch
performance is non-linear and governed by thermal fade (where friction drops as temperature rises),
actuator hydraulic response delay, surface wear, and velocity-dependent dynamic friction curves
($\mu(v_{\text{slip}})$). While our dual-branch architecture utilizes a threshold-based transition
between slipping and locked states to maintain $O(1)$ stack performance and stability at fixed 1 kHz
sub-stepping, the powertrain module is structured to allow dropping in dynamic clamping pressure
and thermal degradation functions in future development phases.
