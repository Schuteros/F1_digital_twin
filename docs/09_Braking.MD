# 9. Braking Dynamics and Longitudinal Traction Limits

Braking force is generated by applying mechanical friction to the rotating wheel assemblies,
producing a retarding force at the tire contact patch directed opposite to the vehicle's motion.
In this 1D framework, retarding force increases linearly with brake demand until it reaches the
tire-road traction boundary, governed by static rolling friction.

---

## 9.1. Wheel-Level Traction Bounding

The braking force developed at an individual axle is limited by the maximum available static
rolling friction between the tire tread and the road surface. Braking force cannot exceed this
physical limit without inducing wheel lockup and kinetic sliding:

$$F_{\text{brake, axle}} = \min\left( F_{\text{demand, axle}}, \, \mu_{\text{peak}} \cdot F_{z, \text{axle}} \right), \tag{9.1.}$$

where:
* $F_{\text{brake, axle}}$ is actual retarding braking force produced at the axle in Newtons (N),
* $F_{\text{demand, axle}}$ is requested mechanical braking force from hydraulic pedal input in Newtons (N),
* $\mu_{\text{peak}}$ is peak static coefficient of longitudinal tire friction which is dimensionless,
* $F_{z, \text{axle}}$ is vertical normal force acting on the axle in Newtons (N).

---

## 9.2. Axle-Specific Normal Force Distribution

Front and rear axles yield distinct maximum braking capacities because their vertical normal
forces ($F_{z, \text{front}}$ and $F_{z, \text{rear}}$) differ based on static weight distribution
and longitudinal load transfer during deceleration:

$$F_{\text{max, front}} = \mu_{\text{peak}} \cdot F_{z, \text{front}}, \tag{9.2.}$$

$$F_{\text{max, rear}} = \mu_{\text{peak}} \cdot F_{z, \text{rear}}, \tag{9.3.}$$

where:
* $F_{\text{max, front}}$ is maximum retarding force limit of the front axle in Newtons (N),
* $F_{\text{max, rear}}$ is maximum retarding force limit of the rear axle in Newtons (N),
* $F_{z, \text{front}}$ is normal vertical force acting on the front axle in Newtons (N),
* $F_{z, \text{rear}}$ is normal vertical force acting on the rear axle in Newtons (N),
* $\mu_{\text{peak}}$ is peak static coefficient of longitudinal tire friction which is dimensionless.

---

## 9.3. Total Longitudinal Retarding Force

The net braking force acting on the vehicle's center of gravity is the direct summation of the
clamped braking forces evaluated independently across both front and rear axles:

$$F_{\text{brake, total}} = F_{\text{brake, front}} + F_{\text{brake, rear}}, \tag{9.4.}$$

where:
* $F_{\text{brake, total}}$ is total longitudinal retarding force applied to the chassis in Newtons (N),
* $F_{\text{brake, front}}$ is clamped braking force developed at the front axle in Newtons (N),
* $F_{\text{brake, rear}}$ is clamped braking force developed at the rear axle in Newtons (N).

---

## 9.4. Architectural Notes and Real-World Limitations

[!WARNING]
This baseline braking model assumes instantaneous hydraulic pressure build-up, ideal brake
bias distribution, and operation strictly up to the static rolling friction point ($\mu_{\text{peak}}$).
It does not currently simulate full dynamic wheel lockup transitions into kinetic sliding
($\mu_{\text{sliding}}$), brake pad thermal fade, or Anti-lock Braking System (ABS) pressure
pulsation routines. By clamping retarding force directly against normal load boundaries,
the module maintains strictly deterministic $O(1)$ stack execution and zero-allocation execution
at fixed 1 kHz sub-stepping, providing a robust baseline for 0–100–0 km/h stopping distance benchmarks.
